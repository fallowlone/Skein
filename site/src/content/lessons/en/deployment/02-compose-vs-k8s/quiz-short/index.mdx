Docker Compose is a single-host process manager: one docker-compose.yml declares your services, networks, and volumes, and docker compose up starts them all on this machine. There is no cluster, so overhead is tiny (~50MB). Kubernetes is a distributed control plane that runs across machines: you do not start containers, you declare a desired state (N replicas, behind this service, with these probes) and the control plane works continuously to make reality match — across nodes.     { q: "What is the reconciliation loop, and why is it the single mechanism that separates Kubernetes from Compose?", a: "The reconciliation loop is the engine of Kubernetes. Controllers continuously watch the cluster, compare actual state against the desired state stored in etcd, and close the gap — event-driven, forever. A ReplicaSet controller's only job is 'keep N pods running': kill a pod and it replaces it; lose a whole node and the scheduler reschedules its pods onto healthy nodes. Compose has no such loop. Its restart: always restarts a container on the same host, but if the host dies, everything on it dies with it. The continuous matching is the whole product you are paying for, and self-healing, gated rolling updates, and autoscaling are all expressions of it." },
    { q: "How does a zero-downtime rolling update with automatic rollback work in Kubernetes, and what does Compose do instead?", a: "A Kubernetes Deployment, driven by the reconciliation loop, brings up new pods, waits for their readiness probe to pass, shifts traffic to them, and only then tears down the old ones. A bad release never serves traffic because unhealthy pods never pass the gate, and the Deployment keeps rollout history so it can roll back automatically. Compose has none of this: it restarts the service in place, and for the window between stop and healthy-start that service is simply down. There is no readiness gate and no automatic rollback — the health gating is exactly the capability the loop adds." },

A credible production setup costs into the low five figures per month, and the larger hidden cost is engineer-hours spent keeping the cluster healthy instead of shipping features. The control plane alone adds roughly 2GB of overhead before your app runs a single container." },

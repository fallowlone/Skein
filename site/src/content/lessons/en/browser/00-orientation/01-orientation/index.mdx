## Three kinds of text become one page

You never hand the browser a finished picture. You hand it three plain-text files, each with a different job:

- **HTML** is the content and structure — the headings, paragraphs, and the button itself.
- **CSS** is the look — colors, spacing, fonts, where things sit.
- **JavaScript** is the behavior — what happens when you click, type, or scroll.

The browser reads the HTML, applies the CSS, and runs the JavaScript, then paints the result on screen. Change any one of the three and the page changes. That is the whole job, repeated thousands of times a second.

## One task at a time

Here is the part beginners almost always get wrong: your JavaScript does not run "in parallel" with everything else. The browser has one main line of work, and it does one task to completion before starting the next — read this chunk, run that click handler, update the screen, then the next thing.

This is why a slow piece of your code freezes the whole page: while it runs, nothing else can. The button does not respond, the scroll locks up, the spinner stops spinning — because the one worker is busy with your task and cannot do two things at once. The system that lines these tasks up and runs them one by one is called the **event loop**, and it is the first real topic of this climb.

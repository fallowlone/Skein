<Hook>
A user signs up and your app must send a welcome email. The email provider takes three seconds per send. You could make the user wait — or you could drop a note saying "send welcome email to this address" into a queue, show the user success immediately, and let a separate worker pick up the note whenever it is ready. This lesson walks that loop once, slowly: send, receive, ack.
</Hook>

<Crux>Using a queue is one loop with three steps: a producer sends a message, a consumer receives and processes it, and the consumer acks it so the broker can delete it.</Crux>

<Explanation>

## The three steps, with one example

Follow a single welcome email through the loop. Each step has exactly one actor and one job.

Notice what each step does NOT do. The producer never sends the email. The consumer never tells the producer it finished — the ack goes to the broker, not back to the sender. And the broker never processes anything; it only holds the message between steps 1 and 3.

## Why the ack is a separate step

Beginners merge steps 2 and 3: "receiving IS finishing." It is not. Imagine the worker reads the message, starts sending the email, and crashes halfway. If receiving counted as done, the broker would have deleted the message — and the user would never get their email, silently.

Because the ack is separate, the crash is harmless: no ack arrived, so the broker still holds the message and hands it to another worker, which sends the email. The price is that the email might occasionally be sent twice — and asking what to do about exactly that is the entire subject of unit `01-delivery-guarantees`, the next unit in this track.

<Inset kind="why" lang="en">
Why doesn't the producer just wait for the ack itself? Because then it would be coupled again — waiting on the consumer is exactly the three-second signup screen this loop removes. The producer's contract ends at step 1: the message is the broker's responsibility now. Waiting, retrying, and confirming all happen between the broker and the consumer, where the producer never sees them.
</Inset>

<KeyTakeaway>Send, receive, ack: the producer publishes and moves on, the consumer processes at its own pace, and the ack — a separate step — is what lets the broker delete the message. No ack, no deletion.</KeyTakeaway>

<Recap lang="en">
Every queue interaction is the same three-step loop. The producer sends a message to the broker and returns immediately. The consumer receives the message whenever it is ready and processes it. Then — as its own step — the consumer acks, and only then does the broker delete the message. A crash before the ack is harmless: the message is still held and gets redelivered. The one thing this loop does not answer is what happens when redelivery sends the same email twice — that question, delivery guarantees, is unit 01, and you now own every word it assumes.
</Recap>

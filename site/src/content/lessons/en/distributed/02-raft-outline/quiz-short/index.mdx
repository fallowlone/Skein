Because no client was ever acknowledged for an uncommitted entry, discarding it loses nothing. The leader signals commitment by advancing its commitIndex and piggybacking it on the next AppendEntries/heartbeat so followers can apply up to that point." },
    { q: "How does the RequestVote log-completeness rule combine with quorum overlap to give Leader Completeness, and what is the extra current-term commit caveat?", a: "A voter grants its vote only if the candidate's log is at least as up-to-date (higher lastLogTerm wins; equal terms break to higher lastLogIndex). Any election quorum overlaps every prior commit quorum, and the shared node will refuse a candidate missing its committed entries — so the winner must hold all committed history. That is Leader Completeness: every leader of term T+1 has every entry committed in terms 1..T. The caveat: a leader may only directly commit an entry from its own current term. Counting replicas to commit a prior-term entry reopens the figure-8 case where a committed-looking entry is later overwritten; prior entries commit indirectly once a current-term entry above them commits." },
    { q: "Contrast ReadIndex and lease reads for serving linearizable reads, and state the correctness precondition that makes lease reads risky.", a: "A naive linearizable read commits a no-op log entry per read — a full consensus round, far too expensive at high read:write ratios. ReadIndex avoids the log write: the leader records its commitIndex, sends one heartbeat round to confirm a majority still recognises it as leader (so a partitioned stale leader cannot serve), waits until its state machine has applied up to that index, then answers — cost is one round-trip, ~1–5 ms intra-region. Lease reads go further: while the leader holds a time-bounded lease (typically election_timeout * 0.9), it serves reads with zero RPCs, sub-millisecond. The risk: lease validity depends on bounded clock skew. If the leader's clock runs ahead, a follower's lease can expire first and elect a new leader while the old one still serves reads — stale data as fresh. So NTP/PTP sync is a correctness requirement for lease reads, not hygiene." },

Joint consensus introduces an intermediate config C_old_new in which every commit and election needs a majority of BOTH the old and the new configuration simultaneously. That guarantees any two valid quorums in the transition overlap. Once C_old_new is committed cluster-wide, the cluster moves to C_new alone. Production systems simplify further to single-server changes (add/remove one node at a time), usually staging new nodes as non-voting learners until they catch up." },

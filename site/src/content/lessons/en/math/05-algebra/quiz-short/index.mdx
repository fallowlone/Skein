You can only combine like terms because each term measures something: 2x means two of whatever x is, while 5 means five plain ones.     { q: "State the balance rule and explain why solving an equation depends on it.", a: "The balance rule says: whatever you do to one side of an equation, do the identical thing to the other side. An equation is a level scale, and its whole claim is that the two sides are equal. If you change one side only — say subtract 1 from the left of 2x + 1 = 9 but leave the right as 9 — you silently break that claim and end up describing a different equation with a different solution. Doing the same move to both sides keeps the scale level, so the variable's value never shifts. You solve by undoing the operations attached to the variable with their inverses — subtraction undoes addition, division undoes multiplication — applied to both sides until the variable stands alone." },

An inequality only asks which side of a boundary the variable is on, and many numbers lie on the same side. Solving x + 3 greater than 7 gives x greater than 4, and that is true for 5, 6, 7, and every number past 4 — the whole range is the answer. <Recap lang="en">
If you could reconstruct each answer from memory, you hold the unit's spine: a variable names a number (unknown or changing); an expression is a recipe you evaluate by substituting and simplify by combining like terms; an equation adds an equals sign and is solved by the balance rule — same move to both sides — undoing operations with inverses; and an inequality uses the same rule but, because it asks which side of a boundary, answers with a whole range. The chain runs from naming a number to pinning it to bounding it.

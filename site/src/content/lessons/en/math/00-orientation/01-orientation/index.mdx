<Hook>
Most people think they are "bad at math." What they usually mean is that someone made them memorize steps without ever saying why the steps work. But you already do math every day: you split a bill, you guess if you have enough time to catch a bus, you notice a pattern. Math is not a secret talent some people are born with. It is a way of reasoning carefully — and like any way of thinking, it gets stronger the more you use it.

<Crux>Math is reasoning from a few agreed rules to conclusions you can be certain of.</Crux>

<Explanation>

## Rules in, certainty out

Every part of math starts with a small set of agreed-upon **rules** — things we simply decide are true to begin with, like "if you add one to a number, you get the next number." From those rules, you reason step by step. Each step follows from the one before it. When you reach the end, the answer is not a guess or an opinion — it is *forced* by the rules. That certainty is what makes math different from almost everything else.

This is why "show your work" matters. The answer alone is not the point; the **chain of reasoning** that gets you there is the real thing math teaches.

- **Rule** — something agreed to be true at the start (also called an axiom or definition).
- **Reasoning** — moving from what you know to what must follow, one careful step at a time.
- **Proof** — a complete chain of reasoning that leaves no room for doubt.
- **Pattern** — a regularity you notice, which often hints at a rule underneath.

## Why memorizing fails and reasoning lasts

If you only memorize "the answer to this kind of problem," then a slightly different problem stops you cold. But if you understand *why* a rule works, you can rebuild the answer even when the problem looks new. That is the whole game. Math feels hard when it is taught as a list of tricks to remember; it feels natural when each step is something you can see has to be true.

You will not need to memorize this track. You will need to follow the reasoning, ask "why does that step hold?", and try things yourself. The certainty at the end is the reward — and it is available to anyone willing to think slowly and carefully.

<Recap lang="en">
Math is not a pile of formulas and it is not an inborn talent. It is reasoning: you start from a few agreed rules, move one careful step at a time, and arrive at a conclusion that must be true. That certainty is what makes math special. Memorizing tricks breaks on new problems; understanding why a step holds lets you rebuild the answer anywhere. One picture to carry up: rules in, careful steps, certainty out. The rest of this track teaches that skill from zero.

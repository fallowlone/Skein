It can never exceed 1 because the favourable outcomes are always part of the total — a part divided by the whole cannot be more than 1, and cannot drop below 0." },

Dependent events are the opposite: drawing a marble from a bag and keeping it, then drawing again, is dependent, because removing the first marble changes what is left and so changes the second draw's probabilities. The test is always: does the first outcome change the possibilities for the second?" },

The result gets smaller because requiring two things to both happen keeps only the overlap — a slice of a slice. Each extra condition cuts the surviving possibilities further, and multiplying two numbers each below 1 always produces something smaller than either one." },

'Both happen' multiplies; 'either of two exclusive events happens' adds. Always check which question you are answering before choosing the operation." },

So the probability of the complement is 1 minus the probability of the event. If the chance of rain is 0.3, the chance of no rain is 1 − 0.3 = 0.7. The complement is often the easiest path to an answer: when 'at least one' is awkward to count directly, computing 1 minus the chance of 'none' is simpler." },

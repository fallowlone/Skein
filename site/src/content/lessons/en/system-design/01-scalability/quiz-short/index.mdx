In the M/M/1 model W = 1/(μ−λ): as offered load λ approaches the service rate μ, the denominator shrinks toward zero and waiting time explodes. At 50% utilization the tail is gentle; at 90% it's ~10× the unloaded time; at 98% ~50×. So you target ~60–70% utilization and keep 30–40% headroom — the last slice of throughput costs unbounded latency, and that's where outages live." },
    { q: "Compare vertical and horizontal scaling, and state what the USL adds beyond Amdahl's law.", a: "Vertical (a bigger machine) is near-free in engineering but hits a hardware ceiling, a superlinear cost curve, and stays a single point of failure. Horizontal (more machines) is elastic and survives node death but requires statelessness (externalize session/cache/file state; sticky sessions are a band-aid). The USL prices horizontal scaling with two terms: α (contention → Amdahl's ceiling, throughput plateaus) and β (coherency → cross-node coordination growing as N(N−1), which makes the curve retrograde — past a peak, more nodes LOWER throughput). Amdahl gives only the plateau; β gives the downturn." },

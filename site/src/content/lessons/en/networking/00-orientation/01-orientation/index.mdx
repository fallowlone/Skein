<Hook>
You type a website address and press Enter. The page appears almost instantly. But your computer is not plugged into that website. There is no single long wire from your laptop to a server on the other side of the planet. So how did your request get there, and how did the answer come back? Something carried your message across a chain of machines you never see. That something is a network.
</Hook>

<Crux>A network carries a message from one computer to another by passing it through many hops.</Crux>

<Explanation>

## There is no single wire

It feels like your computer talks straight to the website. It does not. Between you and the server sit many other machines — your home router, your internet provider's equipment, big routers across cities and oceans. Your message is handed from one machine to the next, again and again, until it reaches the destination. Each handoff is called a **hop**, and each in-between machine that forwards your message is a **router**. No single device knows the whole path. Each one only knows the next step to get a little closer.

## A message travels in small pieces

Your message is not sent as one big blob. The network chops it into small chunks called **packets**. Each packet carries a piece of your data plus an address that says where it is going. The packets travel hop by hop — and they can even take different paths — then get put back together in the right order at the other end. This is why a network is robust: if one route is busy or broken, packets can flow around it.

## What the rest of this pillar covers

Once you accept "message, broken into packets, passed hop by hop," every later topic is a detail of *how that is done well*. How bits actually move on a wire. How a packet's address works. How two machines agree to talk reliably so nothing is lost. How a name like a website turns into an address. How the message is kept private. You do not need any of that yet — just carry the one picture: a message hopping across many machines to reach a computer it is not directly wired to.

<Recap lang="en">
There is no single wire from your computer to a distant server. A network is the machinery that carries your message across a chain of machines: it splits the message into small packets, and each packet is passed hop by hop, with every router knowing only the next step. That one picture — a message hopping across many machines — is the mental model you carry up into the rest of this pillar.

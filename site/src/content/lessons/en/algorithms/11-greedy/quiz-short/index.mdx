(2) Exchange: modify O by swapping out its first differing part and swapping in the greedy choice, producing O', designed so O' is still feasible and no worse than O. (3) Conclude: since O was optimal and O' is no worse, O' is also optimal and now agrees with greedy — so an optimal solution containing the greedy choice exists, and the greedy first choice is safe. The crucial direction: you do not build greedy's answer from scratch, you reshape a known optimum toward it one harmless swap at a time. Then optimal substructure plus induction extends safety to the whole solution." },
    { q: "Why does 'no worse' (rather than 'strictly better') suffice in the exchange step, and why is that the right strength?", a: "You only need O' to be as good as the optimum O, not strictly better. If you could always make it strictly better, then O was not optimal — a contradiction that is unnecessary. The honest claim is gentler: the greedy choice never costs you anything. Since O was optimal and O' matches its value, O' is also optimal and now contains the greedy choice. 'No worse' is strong enough to prove safety and weak enough to actually hold — in activity selection the swap removes one job and adds one, keeping the count exactly equal, which is a successful proof, not a failure." },
    { q: "For interval problems, which sort key goes with which goal, and why is each correct?", a: "For maximizing non-overlapping intervals (activity selection): sort by earliest finish time — the earliest-finishing interval leaves the most room behind it, so by the exchange argument it is always a safe pick. For merging overlapping intervals into the fewest disjoint runs: sort by start time — once you pass an interval nothing later can reach behind the current run's start, so a single left-to-right sweep comparing against one run is correct. For minimum rooms (meeting rooms II): sort starts and ends separately and sweep a two-pointer counter — the answer is the peak number of simultaneously active intervals. Sorting by shortest duration is a wrong greedy for maximization: a short interval in the middle can block two compatible neighbours ([0,4],[3,5],[4,8] gives 1 instead of 2)." },
    { q: "How does 'greedy stays ahead' differ from the exchange argument, and when would you use it?", a: "Both prove the same correctness, but by different mechanics. The exchange argument transforms a known optimum toward greedy by harmless swaps. 'Greedy stays ahead' instead tracks a single measurable quantity and proves greedy's value is at every step at least as good as any other solution's — for activity selection, that after picking its first k jobs greedy's k-th job finishes no later than any other valid schedule's k-th job, so greedy never falls behind and fits at least as many. Use whichever is cleaner: stays-ahead shines when there is a natural monotone quantity to track; the exchange argument is the more general tool when you can design a local swap." },

To find the start, walk once with a running tank since the candidate start; whenever the tank goes negative at station i, reset start to i+1 and tank to 0. The reset is safe because every station between the old start and i was reached with a non-negative running tank, so starting at any of them would reach i with even less fuel and also fail — you lose nothing by skipping them all at once. When total >= 0, whatever start survives the sweep is correct. That is what makes one pass sufficient instead of trying all n starts (O(n^2))." },

<Recap lang="en">
The through-line of the unit is one discipline: a greedy algorithm always runs and always returns something, so the only question that matters is whether its local choice is provably global. The exchange argument (or stays-ahead) is how you prove safety; a missing swap — like coins {1,3,4} for 6 — is itself the proof of failure, and your fallback is DP. For interval problems the hard part is the sort key: earliest finish for maximization, start for merging, start-and-end sweep for rooms. And for scan greedies like Gas Station, never decide from a local dip — decide from the global invariant.
</Recap>

    { q: "Why is tail sampling required in addition to head sampling, and what is the canonical production policy?", a: "Head sampling decides at trace start, before any error or slow tail has appeared, so a probabilistic keep-5% rule discards 95% of the interesting traces up front — and you cannot retrieve spans you never emitted. Tail sampling instead emits 100% at the SDK and buffers all of a trace's spans in the collector until the trace closes (or a ~10–30s decision window expires), then decides with full information. Canonical policy: keep 100% of traces with any ERROR span, keep 100% of traces over a slow threshold (e.g. 2s), keep a 5% random baseline of everything else. This drops trace volume 80–90% while preserving every trace you would ever navigate; the baseline provides rate and pattern context. The cost is collector memory to buffer spans — which is why the collector is tier-0 infrastructure with its own SLO." },

A multi-window alert fires only when a short window (e.g. 5m, for fast detection) AND a long window (e.g. 1h, for confirmation) are both burning above the threshold. The AND suppresses single-spike false positives while keeping MTTD low. A static threshold on raw error rate either pages on every transient blip (too noisy) or is set so high it misses slow burns that quietly drain the budget.

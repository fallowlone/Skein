    { q: "How do the parent chain and the child chain each locate where to apply the fix?", a: "The parent chain answers 'where is this leaf called from?'. Concentrated fan-in (one dominant parent) means the leaf is hammered from a single site — fix the caller (call less, batch, cache, eliminate the call); the classic case is json.Marshal wide under one logger path — fix the logger, not the library. Distributed fan-in (twenty thin parents) means the leaf is shared infrastructure — fix the leaf itself, because optimising one of twenty callers leaves the rest unchanged, and a small leaf win amplifies across all paths. The child chain answers 'does this function do the work or delegate?'. High self-time with narrow children means the work is here — fix inside the function. Thin self-time with wide cum-time means it is a dispatcher — the cost is one level down in a callee. The 'boundary of meaningful work' is the level where cum-time drops sharply going up; fix inside that boundary, never in the dispatch above it." },
    { q: "When do you reach for hardware counters instead of just reading the flame graph, and what do they reveal?", a: "Reach for counters whenever a wide leaf could be either compute-bound or memory-bound — the flame graph shows the function is wide (CPU-time share) but cannot say whether those cycles retired instructions or stalled on memory. perf stat (or VTune / AMD uProf / toplev) gives IPC and cache-miss rates that make the distinction mechanical: IPC 2–4 with a low miss rate is compute-bound (algorithm/SIMD); IPC <1 with a high L3/DRAM miss rate is memory-bound (data layout).

<Crux>Window = max-size OR max-wait. Both needed to avoid starvation (slow producer) or overload (fast producer).</Crux>

<Explanation>
Every production batching system has two independent limits:
- **max-size**: maximum bytes, records, or messages in a batch
- **max-wait**: maximum time to wait before sending a partial batch

The batch closes when EITHER limit is reached, not both. This creates a self-adjusting window that protects against two opposite failure modes:
1. **Slow producer** (few items/second): Without max-wait, the first item waits indefinitely for the batch to fill
2. **Fast producer** (many items/second): Without max-size, batches grow unbounded and overwhelm downstream

Real-world examples:
- Kafka producer: `batch.size` (max-size) + `linger.ms` (max-wait)
- AWS SDK: `maxBatchItems` + `maxBatchWindowMillis`
- io_uring: queue depth (max-size) + polling interval (max-wait)
- TCP Nagle's algorithm: MSS bytes (max-size) + ACK timeout (max-wait)

The senior insight: tune max-size to dominate at peak load (batch fills before timer fires) and max-wait to bound latency during sparse periods. Common defaults: 64 KB - 1 MB size, 10-100 ms wait.

<PersonaTag id="bea" lang="en"/> sets Kafka producer to linger.ms=0 and batch.size=16KB; producer sends immediately, throughput limited by round-trips. <PersonaTag id="sven" lang="en"/> sets linger.ms=10 and batch.size=64KB; producer waits up to 10ms or 64KB, gaining 10-100x throughput with bounded latency penalty.
</Explanation>

<KeyTakeaway>Window = max-size OR max-wait. Both needed to avoid starvation (slow producer) or overload (fast producer). Tune to SLO ceiling, not max throughput.</KeyTakeaway>

<Recap lang="en">
The two-dimensional window (max-size + max-wait) is the universal batching pattern. It self-adjusts to load: during slow periods, time limit dominates; during busy periods, size limit dominates. This prevents both starvation (items waiting indefinitely) and overload (unbounded batch growth). Senior engineers tune max-size for peak load throughput and max-wait for latency SLO compliance.
</Recap>

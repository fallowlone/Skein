It wins when the cheap model succeeds often enough that the average lands well below the flat expensive rate. It backfires two ways: a high escalation rate means you pay the cheap call and then the expensive retry — more than going straight to expensive — and a wrong route silently degrades quality. The classifier itself also adds latency and sometimes its own model call. The rule of thumb: measure the escalation rate, and if it's above ~30% your router or your cheap model is misconfigured." },

On Anthropic, cache reads cost 0.1x the base input rate (a ~90% discount) while the write to populate the cache costs 1.25x (5-minute TTL) or 2x (1-hour TTL). The arithmetic: a 5-minute cache pays for itself after a single hit, a 1-hour cache after the second. To maximise it, order the prompt cache-friendly — stable content first, volatile content (the latest user message, fresh retrieval) last — so the cacheable prefix is as long as possible. <Recap lang="en">
If you could reconstruct each answer from memory, you hold the unit's spine: output costs ~5x input so cap it; the system prompt, history, and RAG all re-send every turn (fixed, linear, and multiplicative respectively); routing saves only at a low escalation rate; caching the stable prefix drops it to 0.1x and pays off on the first hit; and because a runaway loop is superlinear while a monthly cap is measured in days, the real brake is an in-process budget plus a kill switch on cost velocity. Now when you face a real cost incident, you'll reach for the arithmetic before you reach for the cap.
